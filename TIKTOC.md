# TIKTOC.md — Social Media Marketing +1
## AI+1: The Practitioner's Guide to the Delegation Boundary

**Series:** Irreducibly Human: What AI Can and Can't Do
**Author:** Humanitarians AI · ni.brown@neu.edu · Bear Brown & Company
**Book type:** Practitioner handbook
**Primary reader:** Working social media managers and marketing professionals
**Version:** 1.0

---

## Central thesis

AI handles the pattern-shaped tasks in social media marketing —
drafting, scheduling, analytics, content variation, A/B testing —
but the judgment-shaped tasks currently require a human: brand voice
calibration, community trust, crisis reading, relationship decisions,
and audience context. Professionals who cannot draw that boundary are
either burning time on pattern work or eroding the human signal that
made their marketing work in the first place.

---

## Learner profile

**Primary reader:** Working social media managers and marketing
professionals who already use AI tools. They know how to prompt.
They don't have a framework for when not to.

**Prior knowledge assumed:**
- Hands-on experience with at least one AI writing or marketing tool
- Working knowledge of major social platforms
- At least one "ChatGPT for Marketing" book or equivalent exposure

**Prior misconceptions to correct:**
1. More AI = better output (not if it erodes the human signal)
2. The human layer is just editing (it is judgment, not proofreading)
3. AI tools will tell you when they're wrong (they won't)

**Motivation:** Professional (job need) primary. They have hit the
wall where AI output is technically correct but brand-wrong, or where
they suspect they are doing manually what they should be delegating.

## Book type and deployment

**PRIMARY TYPE:** Practitioner handbook — chapters organized by task,
self-contained, non-linear entry.

**Deployment contexts:**
- Reference by task (primary)
- Linear read
- Workshop curriculum (Humanitarians AI)

**The book this is NOT:** Any "ChatGPT for Marketing" prompt library.
Those books tell you how to prompt. This book tells you what deserves

## The AI+1 framework — three nested levels

**Level 1 — Conceptual (why the boundary exists):**
Pattern-shaped vs. judgment-shaped work.
AI executes patterns. Humans provide judgment.

**Level 2 — Operational (where the boundary sits per task):**
Delegate List / Guard List.
Every chapter has both. Explicit. Actionable.

**Level 3 — Execution (how to work the boundary today):**
Do This with AI / Never Do This with AI.
Chapter-level structure. Hands-on. Copy-paste Claude Code prompt.

---

## Chapter anatomy (every chapter)

1. Opening case — a failure or win that makes the boundary visible
2. The pattern-shaped work in this task (Delegate List)
3. The judgment-shaped work in this task (Guard List)
4. Do This with AI — hands-on execution
5. Never Do This with AI — explicit guardrails
6. The +1 — what the human brings that makes AI output usable
7. Claude Code prompt — copy-paste ready, chapter-specific
8. Key terms (5–8, plain language)

---

## Three-act arc

**Act One — Establish the boundary (Chapters 1–2)**
Why the delegation boundary exists. The pattern/judgment distinction.
What AI is structurally good at in social marketing and what it
cannot do. The cost of getting it wrong in both directions.

**Act Two — Task by task (Chapters 3–9)**
Each chapter: one major social marketing task. Delegate List.
Guard List. Do This with AI. Never Do This with AI.
Hands-on Claude Code prompt at chapter end.

**Act Three — Domain and integration (Chapters 10–12)**
Putting the full framework to work. Industry-specific calibration.
Building your own Delegate/Guard map. What changes as AI improves
and what doesn't.

## Field positioning

**The gap this book fills:** No practitioner handbook draws the
delegation boundary for social media marketing with a repeatable
judgment framework, hands-on execution, and domain-specific
calibration. Every competitor is either a prompt library or a
philosophy lecture.

**vs. Any "ChatGPT for Marketing" prompt book:**
"Unlike prompt libraries, which tell you how to use AI tools,
this book tells you what deserves a prompt and what doesn't —
and why the difference matters for your brand."

**vs. Gary Vaynerchuk / tactical social media books:**
"Unlike purely tactical guides, this book gives you the judgment
framework that makes tactics transferable as platforms and tools change."

**vs. Academic social media marketing texts:**
"Unlike academic treatments, this book is organized by the tasks
practitioners actually perform, with explicit AI+1 guidance for each."

## Series positioning

Part of the Irreducibly Human series. Shares thesis architecture
with *AI for Real Estate Professionals* and *Causal Reasoning:
Irreducibly Human*. The Delegate List / Guard List framework mirrors
the Real Estate series. "Currently requires" human involvement is
the accurate empirical framing throughout — not absolutist, not
dismissive of AI capability.

**Shared series thesis:** AI is structurally incapable of
judgment-shaped, relationship-dependent, and accountability-dependent
tasks. "Currently requires" human involvement is the accurate
empirical framing.
