## Figure 4.2 — Parasocial Bond Accumulation and Retroactive Collapse

**Priority: Critical.** This is the chapter's hardest and most counter-intuitive mechanism, and the author note (line 37) requests it. The non-recoverability — that detection retroactively revises every previous interaction at once — is a *temporal* claim that prose alone cannot make felt. Without the figure, readers will treat automated reply as a discrete per-interaction risk rather than a portfolio risk.

### Block 1 — Illustrae paste block

A two-panel side-by-side composition. Left panel: a Cartesian chart, X-axis = number of interactions over time (0 to N), Y-axis = perceived bond strength (0 to 100, baseline at zero). A Bluish Green `#009E73` 2pt line rises in small monotonic steps from 0 to roughly 75 — each step labeled (post-typography) as "perceived choice by human." Small Bluish Green filled circles mark each step. The panel represents normal bond accumulation. Right panel: identical axes, same Bluish Green stepped line rising to roughly 75 — then at the rightmost interaction, a single Vermillion `#D55E00` filled circle marks "automation detected." From that point, a single Vermillion 2pt vertical arrow drops *all the way down to zero* — not gradually, but in a single drop. A second Vermillion 1pt dashed line then sweeps *backward* across the entire prior accumulation curve, retroactively recoloring it Vermillion 50% — the visual claim that the whole history is reinterpreted at once. Below the right panel, a small Vermillion filled rectangle marks "non-recoverable." Between the two panels, a small "vs" placeholder for typography. White background, flat vector, double-column 174mm.
