## Zone Map

- **MC:** Four-cadence practice: daily (classify before task, five-sentence log) → weekly (15–30 min audit) → monthly (45–60 min map revision) → quarterly (3–4 hr re-audit). Minimum viable practice: daily log + monthly map check. Lapse ≠ relapse (Prochaska-DiClemente). The reflection cannot be outsourced; AI can format but not reflect.
- **VG:** Four failure modes (overdesign, outsourced reflection, moralism, tool dependency). Recovery protocol sequence: acknowledge with specificity → correct at equivalent visibility → escalate internally → root-cause within 72 hours → map revision within one week. Three sources: Schön (1983) reflection-on-action; Gollwitzer (1999) implementation intentions; Lally et al. (2009) habit formation 66-day median.
- **PQ:** Priya case: 4-week training; 30% time savings; review skipped from week three; one competitor name in paid LinkedIn at week six. ~70% practice abandonment inside 90 days (Norcross et al.). Lally et al. habit formation ~66-day median. Daily: 5–10 min; weekly: 15–30 min; monthly: 45–60 min; quarterly: 3–4 hours.

## What CAJAL Declines to Architect

- **Four-cadence summary table (line 45).** The table version in the markdown has rows × five columns (time required, core activity, AI helps with, must be human, failure mode if skipped). That five-column reference grid is typography. Figure 12.1's stacked-band visual is structurally different — it shows the time-scale nesting and the minimum-viable bracket, not the row-by-row content. Render both: the table as type, the figure as nested bands.
- **Log-compounding timeline (line 59).** The marker references Month 1 → Month 6 → Year 1 → Year 3 milestones. Declined as a separate figure: this would duplicate the Figure 12.2 longitudinal-record triangle (Orange marker after the 90-day line) without adding mechanism. If the production team wants this as a separate figure, the conceptual content is "what the log reveals at each stage" — but the chapter prose handles this at the right level of abstraction, and a figure version would invite over-specification.
- **Priya case timeline.** The week-by-week narrative (week one Monday, week one Friday, week two, week three, week four, day thirty) is a worked example that earns its weight in prose. A figure version (calendar with annotated days) would over-formalize a narrative case study and clutter the chapter's closing momentum.
- **Recovery protocol flowchart.** Tempting to diagram the five-step recovery sequence (acknowledge → correct → escalate → root-cause → map revision). Declined: the protocol is short enough to live in prose, and a figure version would compete with Figure 10.3 (the map lifecycle cycle) for the reader's process-figure attention.
- **Lapse-vs-relapse spectrum.** Tempting to visualize Prochaska-DiClemente. Declined: the distinction earns its weight as a one-paragraph clarification, not as a clinical-looking spectrum that would import more medical-framing weight than the chapter wants.
- **Habit-formation curve (Lally et al. 66-day median).** Tempting to plot. Declined: the figure would be a single empirical reference that does not do conceptual work in the chapter. The minimum-viable-practice claim is justified by Figure 12.2's adoption curve, not by the habit-formation literature directly.

## Split-point note

Figure 12.1's nested-band layout should coordinate with Chapter 10's map-lifecycle cycle (Figure 10.3). Both figures use cyclic/temporal logic and should feel like sibling diagrams — one shows the map-building cycle, the other shows the practice-sustaining cadence. If the visual treatments diverge, the reader loses the structural rhyme between Chapter 10 and Chapter 12.

Figure 12.3's two-column split is the closing figure of the book. It must read as a final visual summary of the Delegate/Guard canon — verify the Bluish Green/Vermillion color treatment matches the chapter-1 and chapter-2 framework figures exactly. If the canonical Delegate/Guard colors have drifted at all through the book's figure production, this is the figure where the drift will be most visible.

The minimum-viable practice bracket on Figure 12.1 (the Vermillion 1.5pt bracket on the left) is the only place in the chapter's figure set where Vermillion is used as a structural marker rather than as a Guard-list code. Verify this does not create confusion — the reader should read the bracket as "structural floor," not as "Guard task." A small adjustment (Black bracket instead of Vermillion) may improve clarity; flag for review during production.

The chapter closes the book. If the publisher includes a downloadable practice-template PDF, Figure 12.1 (the four-cadence diagram) and Figure 12.3 (the responsibility split) are the two figures most worth licensing or extracting for resource pages.
