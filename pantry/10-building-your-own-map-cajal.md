## Zone Map

- **MC:** Three-axis classification — shape (pattern → judgment), regulatory weight (light → heavy), reputational tail-risk (low → high). The Guard List grows as you move toward the judgment-shaped + heavy-regulated + high-tail-risk corner. Polanyi's Paradox: tacit knowledge means the Guard List built by introspection is incomplete; second-pass articulation required. Map lifecycle: audit → classify → tool audit → draft → boundary cases → trial → revise → socialize → quarterly re-audit.
- **VG:** Three contrast cases (DTC founder, hospital system, SaaS startup) — same audit method, three different maps. Six failure modes (treating map as static, borrowing wholesale, skipping tool audit, AI-written Guard List, outsourced articulation, no compliance involvement). Specific regulatory citations: SEC Marketing Rule, FINRA retainability rules, 18 HIPAA identifiers under 45 CFR Part 164, FTC endorsement rules (2023 update), EU AI Act Articles 50 and 52.
- **PQ:** RIA case: $1.4B AUM; CCO review 45 min → 4 hours; one slipped post live 90 minutes; outside counsel two weeks documenting incident. Three contrast cases: ~$30M DTC apparel, ~$3B hospital system, ~$40M ARR SaaS startup. Audit duration: half a working day; target 30–40 line items in the inventory.

## What CAJAL Declines to Architect

- **Worked-classification line items rendered as typography (line 51).** The classification table in the original markdown lists five tasks (RIA LinkedIn post, hospital disease-awareness, DTC founder post, SaaS thought leadership, internal analytics) with three-axis scores and verdicts. This belongs in the type system as a reference table. The Figure 10.2 calibration matrix captures the *pattern* visually without needing the individual rows to be rendered.
- **RIA failure-cost breakdown.** The opening case includes specific numerics ($1.4B AUM, 45 min → 4 hours, 90-minute exposure, two weeks of outside counsel). These are prose claims; rendering them as a bar chart or pie would over-formalize a narrative case study and steal weight from the conceptual figures.
- **Polanyi's Paradox visualization.** Tempting to diagram tacit knowledge with concentric circles (articulable / tacit / inarticulable). Declined: the prose argument is clear, and a circle diagram would invite over-formalization of a concept whose entire point is that it resists specification. If a future edition wants this figure, scope it as a separate Foundational density addition.
- **Six failure modes (lines 73).** Prose paragraph with concrete remedies attached. Numbered text is the right form. An icon grid version would either be redundant with Figure 10.3 (the lifecycle figure already implies what skipping each step costs) or descend into six labeled icons that read as decoration.
- **CCO Friday-review timeline.** The 45-min-to-4-hour escalation is a one-line claim that earns its weight in prose. A timeline graphic would over-engineer a single data point.

## Split-point note

The series color canon (Bluish Green = Delegate, Vermillion = Guard, Orange = Delegate-with-Review/boundary) is heavily exercised in this chapter — Figure 10.1's corner gradients, Figure 10.2's cell circles, Figure 10.3's central output circles all depend on the reader having internalized the color meaning from earlier chapters. Verify Chapter 2's framework figures establish the canon explicitly. If a reader could open this chapter cold, Figure 10.1 should include a small legend block (post-typography) showing the three Delegate/Guard/boundary swatches.

Coordinate Figure 10.3's cyclical layout with the Chapter 12 daily/weekly/quarterly cadence figure — those are the operational sibling diagrams (one shows the map-building cycle, the other shows the practice-sustaining cycle). If they look visually different in arbitrary ways, the reader will think they're unrelated when they are the same kind of artifact at two different time scales.
